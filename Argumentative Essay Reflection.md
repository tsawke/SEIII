# Argumentative Essay Reflection - SEIII

Drafting the essay through each phase made the final version clearer and more academic. The outline stage helped set a simple structure: an introduction with one thesis sentence and three arguments, followed by body paragraphs and a conclusion. In the first draft, the main ideas were present, but some sentences were unclear and several arguments repeated the same point about “energy.” During peer review and the second draft, the focus shifted to making each argument distinct and adding concrete examples, such as connecting major choice to real job tasks and long-term career commitment. Teacher feedback was most helpful for academic tone and formatting, especially avoiding first and second person, improving transitions, and putting the thesis at the end of the introduction. In the final draft, grammar, punctuation, and word choice were corrected, while the original meaning and organization were mostly kept.

AI tools were used mainly for language accuracy and requirement-checking. ChatGPT 5.2 Thinking was used to fix typos, improve punctuation, and adjust awkward phrases without changing the main ideas. It was also used to reduce similarity between Arguments 2 and 3 by adding different types of support. In addition, AI helped integrate sources smoothly using phrases like “according to” and “research from,” so the essay included evidence instead of only opinion.

This experience showed that strong writing depends on both ideas and revision. Small edits can greatly improve clarity, and evidence makes an argument more convincing. In the future, improvement will come from outlining first, revising in rounds, reading sentences aloud for fluency, and building vocabulary for academic tone.
